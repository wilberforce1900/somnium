\begin{abstract}
\noindent We train a small non-Transformer world model, \som{}, under a developmental curriculum: \emph{chaos $\rightarrow$ dream $\rightarrow$ wake}. A single recurrent substrate carries two dynamics---fast streaming expansion (\emph{yang}) and energy-relaxation contraction (\emph{yin})---softly mixed by a gate; prediction happens in latent space (JEPA-style next-latent), and training begins with a \emph{dream phase} of self-generated rollouts before any real environment interaction. Dream-first pretraining reliably cuts the real-interaction steps needed to reach a fixed accuracy by ${\sim}60\%$: replicated across five independent runs at 16K parameters (ratios 0.39--0.42 against a pre-registered threshold of 0.6). A scale ladder, a dose-scaling control, and autonomous growth runs converge on the same conclusion: the advantage is \emph{capacity-bound}. This paper then documents what happened when we tried to break that boundary and, in the process, broke our own instruments instead. A five-hypothesis exclusion chain traced the wide-model pathology ($d_h \ge 256$) to optimization geometry---not limited expressivity---and a single output LayerNorm cures it (plateau MSE $1.03 \rightarrow 0.17$ at $d_h{=}1024$; the width cliff flattens to $0.115$--$0.175$ across 64--1024). Two instrument defects surfaced and were repaired under pre-registration: JEPA mean-squared error is a \emph{moving yardstick} (encoder target scale drifts $11\times$ across configurations; we switch to scale-invariant decode fidelity), and freshness-only replay causes systematic recency drift (fixed by mixed replay: $0.415 \rightarrow 0.011$ at 9000 cycles). Re-running the central question on the repaired instrument \emph{re-establishes the capacity-bound conclusion in cleaner form}: the dream advantage survives only at the width where wake itself is capacity-limited ($d_h{=}64$; ratios 0.58/0.69), vanishes at 256--1024 ($0.74$--$0.98$ or unreached), and the transferable component is \emph{replay}---once every schedule has replay access, the full dream battery adds nothing. A final pre-registered campaign measured the boundary from the world's side: across four worlds of increasing structural and adversarial depth, the wide-model benefit saturates at ${\sim}3$pp---the world family, not the architecture, is now the binding constraint---and a non-stationary extension (hidden regime switches where the reward semantics of two cells swap) was closed honestly after its instrument iterations showed the base phenomenon (terminal-reward semantics) cannot yet be established at this scale---\emph{a closure this update retracts}: run on a corrected budget, terminal semantics establish at every scale (capacity and reward-loss weighting act as independent ${\sim}5.4\times$ and ${\sim}2\times$ rate knobs), re-invert after a regime flip an order of magnitude faster than they establish, and decay under continuous switching along a forgetting curve with that same time constant. The dream advantage's domain is then drawn precisely---a sub-threshold establishment head start (${\sim}1.4\times$, with $3.5\times$ tighter seed scatter) exactly where wake is capacity-limited, and no measurable contribution to inversion or maintenance---measured over 24 post-birth runs, including an instrument unit-error episode whose flawed matrix is retained as the figure's lower bound. The same boundary holds on the protection side: under a mirrored-observation conflict, forgetting is constructible ($1.4$--$1.7\times$ representational degradation, semantics intact) and is halved by replaying old data but not by dream pretraining; and under observation noise the uncertainty head's coverage collapses ($0.19$--$0.61$) with a terminal-event hole that blind-spot-weighted dream replay fails to repair (a registered instrument defect and a seed-split null close that line). Finally, the external control this line of work invites: a parameter-matched autoregressive transformer, trained lockstep on bit-identical batches, dominates both teacher-forced one-step (${\sim}25$pp) and 16-step open-loop ($51$--$82$pp) observation prediction---leaving \som{} one registered residue (slower error compounding, $4.4$--$4.9\times$ vs.\ $5.8$--$6.8\times$) and no open claim on this scale's observation-prediction bench. All hypotheses, thresholds, corrections, and outcomes---including the retraction of our own earlier ``expressivity ceiling'' claim and, now, of the non-stationary closure itself---are logged in a public run registry. A theory that can be killed is a theory that can evolve.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Should a model meet the world awake or asleep? Mammalian development suggests an ordering: spontaneous, self-generated activity (retinal waves, hippocampal replay) precedes and shapes later sensorimotor learning~\cite{blankenship2009,wilson1994}. Sleep research offers candidate mechanisms---synaptic homeostasis~\cite{tononi2014}, reverse learning~\cite{crick1983}, consolidation via replay~\cite{rasch2013}. In machine learning, world models trained by latent imagination~\cite{hafner2023} and self-supervised objectives in representation space~\cite{lecun2022,assran2023} echo pieces of this story, but the developmental question---\emph{does a model that dreams before it wakes learn more efficiently from its later waking data?}---is rarely asked directly.

We built \som{} to ask it under a discipline borrowed from clinical research: \textbf{pre-registered ablations with a public outcomes registry}. Every claim in this paper was registered with a threshold before the run that tested it; every negative result is retained and reported. The contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Architecture} (\S\ref{sec:arch}). A non-Transformer world model: one recurrent substrate with dual dynamics (streaming expansion / energy relaxation), gate-mixed; JEPA-style next-latent training with $O(1)$ state; a function-preserving width-growth operator; parameter count $3d_h^2 + O(d_h)$.
\item \textbf{A replicated, capacity-bound positive result} (\S\ref{sec:exp}). Dream-first pretraining reaches a wake-only model's final accuracy with ${\sim}40\%$ of the real interactions, replicated $5\times$ at 16K parameters across two compute platforms. A scale ladder (16K/210K/3.2M), a dose-scaling control ($4\times$/$16\times$ dream budget), and autonomous growth runs independently agree: the advantage is real but \emph{capacity-bound}, consistent with its mechanism being energy smoothing---an initialization effect that large models do not need.
\item \textbf{A registry of pre-registered negative results} (\S\ref{sec:neg}). VQ situation codebooks hurt training; learned gating gives no accuracy gain (but its polarity is an emergent developmental marker: a one-way ratchet from corrective to feed-forward regimes); dream-statistics feedback does not improve calibration.
\item \textbf{An autonomous overnight protocol} (\S\ref{sec:night}) in which the model trains itself through self-probes, expands its own width by dreaming, and---in the second night---detects its own numerical divergence and rolls back to its last healthy self.
\end{enumerate}

We emphasize what this paper is \emph{not}: \som{} is a 16K--3.2M-parameter model on procedural gridworlds. Nothing here claims general-purpose intelligence. The point is methodological: with pre-registration and honest registries, a two-day single-GPU project can produce claims that replicate, boundaries that are measured rather than assumed, and failures that accumulate into design knowledge.

\section{Architecture}\label{sec:arch}

\subsection{One substrate, two dynamics}

\som{} has no attention and no token sequence. Its core is a single latent state $h_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}$ updated once per environment step by soft-mixing two dynamics that \emph{share all parameters} (a deliberate contrast with dual-network designs; cf.\ the finding that a single tiny recursive network outperforms a biologically-inspired hierarchy~\cite{jolicoeur2025}):

\begin{align}
h_{t+1} &= h_t + \alpha_t\,\Delta^{\text{yang}}_t + (1-\alpha_t)\,\Delta^{\text{yin}}_t \label{eq:mix}\\
\Delta^{\text{yang}}_t &= \mathrm{dt}\cdot\tanh\!\big(W_c [h_t; e(o_t); a_t]\big) \label{eq:yang}\\
\Delta^{\text{yin}}_t &= -\mathrm{lr}\cdot\nabla_{h} E(h)\big|_{h=h_t}, \qquad \alpha_t = \sigma\!\big(g([h_t; x_t])\big) \label{eq:yin}
\end{align}

\noindent where $e(\cdot)$ is an encoder shared with the training target, $W_c$ a linear cell, $E$ a small MLP energy head, and $g$ a linear gate initialized at $\sigma(0)=0.5$ (born half-and-half). Equation~\eqref{eq:mix} is the model's entire per-step computation: the recurrent state is $O(1)$, ``thinking longer'' costs compute but never parameters, and the parameter count is $3d_h^2 + 52d_h + 296$ to leading order for our 8-dimensional observations and 4 actions (exact counts, including the output LayerNorm, gate, and energy head: $16{,}048$ at $d_h{=}64$ and $3{,}201{,}328$ at $d_h{=}1024$; the leading-order formula errs by $+0.9\%$ and $+0.06\%$ respectively---v1.5 erratum of the approximate figures $16$K/$3.2$M).

\subsection{Training objective}

Training is JEPA-style prediction in latent space, never next-token and never pixel reconstruction:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \big\|h_{t+1} - \mathrm{sg}\big(e(o_{t+1})\big)\big\|^2
+ \lambda_r \big(\hat r_{t+1} - r_{t+1}\big)^2
+ \lambda_v \, \mathcal{N}\!\big(\hat r_{t+1}, \hat\sigma^2_{t+1};\, r_{t+1}\big)
+ \lambda_d \big(\hat o_{t+1} - o_{t+1}\big)^2 ,
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{sg}(\cdot)$ a stop-gradient on the target (v0; no EMA teacher yet), $\hat\sigma$ a heteroscedastic uncertainty head used only for probes and abstention experiments, and $\hat o$ a low-weight observation decode used only to close the loop for planning probes. Each training sequence begins from a noise sample (the ``chaos'' stage): $h_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$.

\subsection{The dream phase}\label{sec:dreamphase}

Before the first real interaction (\emph{dream-first}) the model runs self-generated rollouts with four independently togglable components: \textbf{imagination} (latent rollouts from the noise prior; the effective term turns out to be energy smoothing, \S\ref{sec:disc}), \textbf{consolidation} (replay distillation from the episode buffer), \textbf{reverse learning} (noise-augmented replay, after~\cite{crick1983,shin2017}), and \textbf{rehearsal} (oversampling rare transitions). Three schedules are compared throughout: \emph{wake-only}, \emph{dream-first} (dream pretraining, then wake-only), and \emph{alternate} (a dream phase after each wake cycle). A fairness note applies to all comparisons: dream schedules receive extra offline gradient steps; the metric is always \emph{real environment interactions}.

\subsection{Function-preserving growth}

To let the model widen itself, we use a Net2Net-style operator~\cite{wei2016}: new latent dimensions copy a random source dimension in all \emph{producer} weights (encoder output, cell output) while all \emph{consumer} weights receive zero columns. At zero noise the update map is bit-exact before and after growth (verified in tests); with small noise on new rows only, perturbation is $O(\varepsilon)$ and new dimensions wake up through gradients. Growth multiplies the dream budget by 1.5 (``the dream deepens as the dreamer grows''); the optimizer is rebuilt at each growth event.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Protocol}

Environments are procedural gridworlds (5--12 cells; 8-dim observations: position, goal and hazard offsets, clock; slippery actions; rare hazard teleports) and DFS mazes. All comparisons hold the wake data stream identical across schedules. The primary metric is the \emph{steps-to-target ratio}: real interaction steps for a schedule to first reach the wake-only model's final accuracy, divided by the wake-only total. Thresholds were committed before each run: ratio $\le 0.6$ (i.e., a ${\ge}40\%$ saving) counts as positive. Code, registry, and per-run records: \url{https://github.com/wilberforce1900/somnium}.

\subsection{Dream-first cuts real interactions by 60\% (at 16K)}

In the base configuration ($d_h{=}64$, ${\sim}5.3$K real steps total), dream-first reaches wake-only's final latent MSE using 39\%, 39\%, and 42\% of the real interactions (three seeds; final MSE 0.235 vs.\ 0.905 at equal steps). A cross-platform replication on an L4 GPU reproduced 0.41/0.39 with the identical configuration---a bit-exact determinism check. The \emph{alternate} schedule shows the same direction (0.77) without passing threshold: the benefit concentrates in \emph{pre-training}, not in interleaving. Component decomposition (six variants, pre-registered) attributes the sequential-learning benefit to replay components (consolidation $+6.1$pp, reverse learning $+6.0$pp, rehearsal $+5.3$pp on old-task improvement) while imagination contributes nothing there---its contribution is confined to the birth-time sample efficiency above, a division of labor we summarize as \emph{imagination is for being born; replay is for keeping what waking taught}.

\subsection{The advantage is capacity-bound}\label{sec:scale}

Figure~\ref{fig:scale} shows the scale ladder ($d_h \in \{64, 256, 1024\}$, two seeds each): ratios 0.41/0.39, 0.88/0.79, 0.88/0.53. Two confounds were closed by pre-registered controls:

\textbf{Dose.} Because the dream budget was fixed at 200 phases while width grew, we re-ran with dose $\propto d_h$ (200/800/3200). Outcome: unchanged at 210K (0.88/0.79, bit-identical trajectories' summary values), and at 3.2M the $16\times$ dose \emph{never reaches} the target (slightly worse than fixed dose). Under-dosing is not the explanation.

\textbf{Task saturation.} Wake-only final accuracy is flat in width (MSE 0.89/0.98/1.00): the environment family saturates near a few hundred dimensions, compressing any sample-efficiency advantage.

\subsection{Dream-grown models hit the same wall}\label{sec:night}

In autonomous overnight runs the model chose its own environment difficulty from four self-probes (normalized residual $\resnorm = \|h - e(o')\|/\sqrt{d_h}$, uncertainty $\hat\sigma$, coverage, gate polarity $\alpha$) and grew its width after two consecutive improving rounds. Night~1 (871 rounds, no safeguards) grew healthily from 64 through 486 to 729 ($\resnorm$ band 0.29--0.60), destabilized while at 729 (the visible spikes of Figure~\ref{fig:night}), and the subsequent step to 1024 exploded the residual a further $6.8\times$ (off-axis in the figure); 667 subsequent rounds of self-repair halved it without recovery, with the gate pinned at the corrective pole ($\alpha \approx 0$) throughout. Night~2 (guard rails: growth only when $\resnorm < 0.6$, an anti-starvation rule for environment choice, gradient clipping, and rollback-to-last-healthy-self on divergence) grew to 729 while healthy, explored the environment grid to its ceiling, and---after an unclipped divergence late in the night---\emph{detected the failure via its own $\hat\sigma \to \infty$ probe and restored itself from its round-800 checkpoint}. Night~2 was then extended and ran to 4{,}932 rounds total (night 1's $5.7\times$): at $d_h{=}1024$ the model trained \emph{stably for 3{,}000+ rounds} inside the $\resnorm$ band 0.57--0.81, slowly converging to 0.73 with zero rollbacks---the clipping and guards fully cured night 1's failure mode at the same scale. Figure~\ref{fig:night} shows both trajectories.

\textbf{The wall moves: from expressivity to optimization geometry.} Three probe experiments first triangulated where the wall sits: the flat scale ladder (\S\ref{sec:scale}); a 90-cycle plateau test at $d_h{=}1024$ ($3\times$ budget) confirming the flatness is not under-training; and deepening the BPTT horizon ($T \in \{8,32,64\}$) \emph{degrading} training monotonically (0.67/1.37/2.80). A pre-registered exclusion chain then eliminated the remaining suspects one by one---training budget, BPTT length, encoder rank, learning rate, and cell expressivity (a three-cell comparison: GRU-style gating 1.081, two-layer 1.017, vs.\ linear 1.030; best improvement $1.2\%$)---leaving optimization geometry itself. The decisive experiment was the cheapest: normalizing the latent state at the \emph{output} of the mixed update (width-free, no new state; LayerNorm~\cite{ba2016layer} or RMSNorm~\cite{zhang2019rmsnorm}). Figure~\ref{fig:norm} shows the outcome at $d_h{=}1024$: output LayerNorm reaches $0.166$ and RMSNorm $0.197$ against the pathological floor $1.03$ (threshold for ``cured'': $\le 0.30$, registered before the run; the $d_h{=}64$ reference is $0.134$), while \emph{input-side} normalization (1.036) does nothing---the pathology lives in the \emph{write side}, the accumulation of state, not in what the cell reads. The mechanism panel explains why: without normalization the gate \emph{collapses} early ($\alpha \to 0.016$: the yang pathway shuts off; the yin step is two orders of magnitude smaller) and the state stays frozen at its birth scale---the ``floor'' was simply the variance of the spawn distribution, never the target's. Output normalization releases the trap: the gate recovers ($\alpha \to 0.999$), the state scale becomes a learned quantity that tracks the target manifold, and the width cliff flattens ($0.175/0.115/0.166$ at $d_h = 64/256/1024$, vs.\ $0.134/0.957/1.030$ without). One caveat is kept on the books: RMSNorm's cure is seed-sensitive ($0.197$ vs.\ $0.372$; a second seed falls back into the collapse trap and escapes late), so LayerNorm is the robust fix. \emph{We therefore retract the earlier claim that a single linear cell imposes a hard ceiling near a few hundred dimensions}: the $d_h \le 256$ applicability domain was an artifact of a fixable optimization defect. The five negative probes stand as facts; the inference drawn from them did not.

\subsection{A moving yardstick: converged protocols and scale-invariant metrics}\label{sec:yardstick}

The healing forced an audit of the measuring instruments themselves, with two findings that change how every number in this paper should be read. First, \textbf{JEPA mean-squared error is a moving yardstick}: the encoder defines its own target space, and that space's scale drifts during training (per-dimension target variance differs by $11\times$ across converged configurations). Cross-configuration comparisons of raw latent MSE are therefore meaningless; we re-anchor on \emph{decode fidelity}---the $R^2$ of decoding the next observation from the latent state---against a copy-the-current-frame baseline. Second, \textbf{short budgets measure ``not started''}: a budget bridge at $d_h{=}256$ on a rich world (partial observations, key--door dynamics, four rooms) shows JEPA $R^2$ at $-48\%$ after 120 cycles (worse than predicting the dataset mean), $+64\%$ after 1{,}000, and decode $R^2$ climbing $51\% \rightarrow 87\% \rightarrow 93\%$ (saturating near 4{,}000 cycles). Every ``plateau'' reported by the 120-cycle calibration protocol was a snapshot taken before learning began; all v1.0/v1.1 absolute MSE values are retained in the registry as historical record under this warning.

On the repaired instruments the width question re-opens cleanly. Long runs (9{,}000 cycles, ${\sim}12$M interactions) on the richest world show wide margins: the $d_h{=}1024$ model descends to $0.017$ latent MSE where its 120-cycle snapshot read $0.138$, and decode fidelity reaches $93\%$---seven to ten times better than a frame-copy baseline ($30\%$), i.e.\ genuine next-state prediction, not persistence. The width ladder at convergence reads $92.6\%$ ($d_h{=}256$) $\approx$ $93.2\%$ ($1024$) $>$ $90.2\%$ ($2048$): width parity across 256--1024, with 2048 a slow starter (120-cycle MSE $0.96$, i.e.\ the same pathology signature) that converges normally---not a trap. A second instrument defect surfaced in these long runs: with freshness-only replay (sampling only the newest episodes), the $d_h{=}1024$ model systematically drifts toward recent data ($0.118$ at mid-training $\rightarrow 0.415$ at the end, reproduced across seeds). A pre-registered fix---mixed replay, half fresh windows and half uniform over the whole buffer---cures it ($0.011$ final; gate value $0.20$ below the $0.30$ failure bound, $39\times$ below the drift itself). Mixed replay is the standard protocol for everything that follows.

\section{Negative results registry}\label{sec:neg}

All thresholds were pre-registered; all results retained.

\textbf{A1 --- 64-entry VQ situation codebook (``hexagram'' discrete planning layer).} Motivated by treating situations as a discrete transition graph over which a slow planner searches (value iteration over 64 codes, Xiantian binary encoding). Outcome at two seeds: the \emph{baseline without a codebook} reaches 0.406 success on long-horizon navigation while every codebook configuration scores $\le 0.094$; ablations show the VQ commitment loss \emph{damages the continuous model during training}. Two planner implementations (direct code scoring; potential-shaped search~\cite{ng1999}) failed a pre-launch check against an equal-compute control. Demoted to a read-only interpretability layer.

\textbf{A2 --- learned dual-mode gating.} Learned gate vs.\ fixed single-mode at equal parameters: $+0.9$pp / $-0.8$pp on two tasks against a $+2$pp threshold. The gate \emph{learns} (it settles at $\alpha \approx 0.3$) but converts to no accuracy. The interesting residue is what the gate encodes: polarity is \emph{co-adapted} (forcing a wake-trained model to the yang pole at inference inflates prediction error $4.4\times$), and it behaves as a \textbf{one-way developmental ratchet}---both schedules slide to the corrective (yin) pole early in training ($\alpha \to 0.17$); dream-pretrained models then ratchet to the feed-forward pole ($\alpha \to 0.96$) while wake-only models stay corrective; injecting a new task does \emph{not} reopen the gate. The gate is a stage marker, not an online ``do-I-know-this'' meter: a falsification we ran before believing it.

\textbf{A5 --- reading dreams to guide sampling.} Feeding dream-log statistics back (coverage-blind-spot bias of imagination; inverse-coverage-weighted replay) against matched controls: first implementation was mechanically inert (the imagination objective is content-insensitive); the second verifiably changed sampling but produced \emph{zero} calibration gain. Root cause: the uncertainty head is calibrated out of the box (95\% interval coverage 95--97\%), leaving no headroom to improve; per-window error discrimination was unstable across seeds ($-0.12$ vs.\ $+0.72$). Demoted to an observation layer. \emph{v1.5 closure}: the ``no headroom'' premise itself was later falsified under observation noise ($\sigma_n{=}0.2$ collapses coverage to $0.19$--$0.61$; \S\ref{sec:life}), which re-opened the question---and a pre-registered three-arm re-test (blind-spot-weighted replay vs.\ uniform control vs.\ no-dream) closed it again with a registered instrument defect: the weighting under test turned out not to be gated by the blind-spot flag (the two arms ran bit-identical), and the repair signal split across seeds ($+0.33$/$-0.02$). What survives is a residue consistent with everything else in this paper: on one seed, the \emph{replay volume} itself (not the reading) lifted coverage from $0.61$ to $0.94$. Final status: calibration has headroom under noise; reading dreams does not repair it.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}

\textbf{Mechanism.} Ablations localize the dream-first benefit to the energy-smoothing term of imagination (the anti-collapse variance floor was provably never active): pretraining flattens the energy landscape that the yin dynamics descend, i.e., it shapes the geometry the wake phase will optimize in. This is an initialization effect, which is exactly why it is capacity-bound---large models buy the same geometry with data. The instrument-repaired replication (\S\ref{sec:a3redo}) sharpens this twice over: the margin survives only at the width whose wake phase is itself capacity-limited, and the interleaved dream battery never beats wake training that merely has replay access---the transferable component of dreaming here is \emph{replay}, and imagination's residue is a birth-time margin for small models. The alignment with sleep theory is comfortable but we state it flatly: what we implement is energy smoothing plus replay~\cite{tononi2014,crick1983,shin2017}; no claim about consciousness is made or implied, and we regard ``AGI as the awakening of silicon life'' as poetry, not prediction.

\textbf{Boundaries.} Toy environments; two seeds at scale (with pre-registered extension arms where seeds split); compute asymmetry between dream and wake schedules is documented rather than eliminated; the registry contains two infrastructure failures (a shell incompatibility that produced a false completion, and an unbounded-checkpoint disk exhaustion) that consumed real hours and are reported because they will consume someone else's. The external architecture control this paper long lacked (\S\ref{sec:external}) has now been run---parameter-matched, lockstep, and negative in both teacher-forced and open-loop regimes at this scale. One further boundary is self-inflicted and worth stating plainly: every absolute MSE value in \S\ref{sec:exp} was produced by the pre-repair instrument (under-trained budgets, drifting yardstick, freshness-only replay); we retain them as registered history and read only their relative structure.

\textbf{The ceiling as a finding---and its correction.} We initially read the capacity wall as a limitation of the environments, designed richer ones to escape it, then---when the wall persisted---read it as an expressivity ceiling of the single linear cell. Both readings were wrong, and the correction is itself part of the record: a five-hypothesis exclusion chain (all negative) followed by a four-arm normalization study (two cures, one inert arm localizing the defect to the write side) moved the wall from \emph{expressivity} to \emph{optimization geometry}, retracted the $d_h \le 256$ applicability claim, and restored wide models to small-model accuracy with one width-free layer. We keep the section heading's original claim in the registry rather than editing it away, because the sequence is the point: pre-registered claims are cheap to state, cheap to falsify, and---as here---cheap to correct in public. What survives the correction is sharper than what preceded it: the dream advantage tracks the model--world capacity match with a precision the old instrument could not see.

\textbf{What would change our mind.} The capacity-bound claim remains falsifiable in both directions. A world whose decode-fidelity ladder keeps rising with width at converged protocol would re-open the question of whether dreaming returns when there is structure worth dreaming about; we measured exactly this across a pre-registered depth ladder (\S\ref{sec:kfamily}) and the answer, within this environment family, is no. The non-stationary axis---the sleeping brain's home turf---was closed once by a base-phenomenon gate and then re-opened by the discovery that the gate itself was a budget artifact (\S\ref{sec:nonstat}); it now sits \emph{measured}: establishment, inversion and maintenance are characterized, and after birth none of them responds to the dream schedule. The external yardstick (\S\ref{sec:external}) is similarly two-sided: what would change our mind is a regime---larger scale, richer observations, longer horizons than 16---where the latent substrate beats parameter-matched autoregression on its own compound-prediction terms, not more tuning of the current one.

\subsection{The view from the world's side: four worlds, one ceiling}\label{sec:kfamily}

With the instrument repaired, the ``does the world feed wider bodies'' question becomes measurable: run the converged width ladder on progressively deeper worlds and watch the 256$\to$1024 decode-fidelity gradient. We pre-registered the ladder with a re-open gate ($\ge$10\% relative improvement) and two escalation worlds. The result is a clean saturation curve---$+0.6$, $+0.6$, $+3.4$, $+3.1$pp across four worlds (static; ordered goal sequences; a patrolling hazard; two cooperating patrolling hazards)---never approaching the gate, while the 64$\to$256 gradient explodes ($+38.6$pp at the hardest world; the 64-dimensional body collapses to 45.7\% decode fidelity with its target-space rank crushed from the typical 6--7 to 2). Two boundaries emerge with unusual precision: the world family sets the \emph{floor} of the healthy width band (256, raised by adversarial depth), and the architecture sets its \emph{ceiling} (1024, ${\sim}87$--95\% decode fidelity everywhere). Ordered goal sequences also raise the target-space rank (90\% energy in 9 dimensions vs.\ the typical 6--7)---world structure shows up in the geometry of the learned representation, exactly as a world model should allow. We read the saturation, not the single largest value, as the finding: adversarial depth bought width value exactly once, then stopped.

\subsection{Reopening the non-stationary axis: establishment, inversion, maintenance}\label{sec:nonstat}

The axis sleep theory cares about most---dreaming under \emph{changing} worlds---was closed in v1.3 on a pre-registered ``cannot be established'' verdict for terminal-reward semantics ($0.51$--$0.55$ sign accuracy; the balanced-exposure arm changed nothing). We retract that closure: it was a budget artifact of the same kind the moving-yardstick audit had just taught us to expect. The identical 600-cycle protocol passes the establishment gate at $d_h{=}1024$ on both seeds ($0.862$/$0.873$), and at the original $d_h{=}64$ the semantics establish too---they need ${\sim}2{,}400$ cycles, not 600 ($0.51$--$0.54 \rightarrow 0.85$--$0.86$; a $4{,}800$-cycle extension reaches $0.92$ with a stable asymptote). Everything below is measured with the corrected clock.

\textbf{Establishment} is slow, and rate-limited by capacity and reward weight. Crossing time to the $0.85$ gate (rolling ten-eval mean, two--three seeds) spans ${\sim}2{,}100$ cycles at $d_h{=}64$, ${\sim}1{,}000$ at 256, and ${\sim}390$ at 1024---a $5.4\times$ ladder; up-weighting the reward loss ($\lambda_r \times 4$) is an independent ${\sim}2\times$ accelerator at 16K (with better value accuracy, end MAE $0.94 \rightarrow 0.74$--$0.78$) and adds \emph{nothing} at 256: the two knobs feed one bottleneck and do not compose. Dream-first enters here, and only here: at 16K it accelerates establishment (three-seed paired crossing ratios $0.58$/$0.77$/$0.77$, mean $0.71$---directionally consistent, below the pre-registered $2\times$ bar) and tightens the seed scatter $3.5\times$ ($545 \rightarrow 155$ cycles), while at 256 and 1024 it is inert ($0.91$, $1.16$). The capacity signature repeats a fourth time---now on semantics, not behavior.

\textbf{Inversion} is fast and schedule-blind. After a single flip (the two cells exchange reward polarity), the reward head reads the \emph{old} sign---flip-point sign accuracy $0.05$--$0.16$, i.e.\ confidently wrong---then re-inverts to $0.94$--$0.96$ within the $2{,}400$-cycle post phase: $\tau_{50} = 25$--$70$ cycles, $\tau_{90} = 90$--$215$, an order of magnitude faster than establishment. Whether the model was born with dream-first changes none of this (paired ratios $1.00$/$0.79$; $r_{\tau_{50}} = 0.89$). Once the world is encoded, re-reading the reward is a small retraining problem---and the birth-time advantage does not follow it there.

\textbf{Maintenance} is a forgetting curve with the same constant. Under continuous switching (per-step flip probability $\lambda = 1/\mathrm{dwell}$), in-regime accuracy degrades cleanly: dwell $750$ cycles ($5\tau_{90}$) maintains $0.92$--$0.94$; dwell $150$ ($\tau_{90}$) is partial and seed-unstable ($0.95$/$0.72$); dwell $50$ ($\approx\tau_{50}$) drops to $0.66$--$0.70$---far above chance. Figure~\ref{fig:switch} shows the cliff. Its grey floor is an accident we keep: the first version of this matrix mis-specified dwell's unit (steps instead of cycles, a ${\sim}180\times$ error, diagnosed via its own switch counts: $176$--$183$ steps/cycle). All three of its dwells sat deep in the faster-than-$\tau$ regime and measured chance---flat across $15\times$---supplying the curve's lower bound. Across both matrices ($24$ runs), no dwell shows a stable scheduling effect ($+0.02$, $-0.09$ with a seed split, $+0.02$). The proposal that motivated this axis anticipated three outcomes (the dream advantage persists, decays, or peaks at moderate switching rates); the measured fourth is that the switching rate does not interact with the schedule at all.

\subsection{The dream's domain: birth only}\label{sec:life}

Three independent axes now bound the dream advantage. \emph{Phase}: it lives in pretraining, not interleaving (\S\ref{sec:a3redo}---the full battery never beats wake-with-replay). \emph{Capacity}: it lives only where wake is capacity-limited---64 in the ladder, absent at 256/1024, whether the readout is behavioral steps-to-target or semantic establishment (\S\ref{sec:nonstat}). \emph{Life-stage of the semantics}: it lives only at birth---after establishment, neither inversion nor maintenance depends on it (three post-birth campaigns, three nulls; the single borderline scheduling differential, at the boundary dwell, is seed-split and reads as noise). What dream-first leaves behind is a $1.4\times$ establishment head start, steadier seeds, and better-calibrated values---bought with 200 offline phases against thousands of real cycles: a small, cheap, precisely bounded gift. ``Imagination is for being born; replay is for keeping what waking taught'' now covers semantics as well as behavior: what is born is not only a better-conditioned energy landscape but a faster-to-read reward structure---at exactly the widths where holding values is hard. Whether the gift grows (more capacity, richer reward channels, longer semantic lives) is left as a registered open question, not an argument.

Two final campaigns close the loop from the protection side. \textbf{Forgetting is constructible, and protection is replay's}: a mirrored-observation conflict (the $x$-coordinates of all observations flipped mid-training---same task, opposite representation) degrades old-coordinate decoding by $1.4$--$1.7\times$ (a pre-registered retention gate, passed on both seeds) while reward semantics on the old coordinates \emph{improve}---forgetting here is representational, not semantic. Against that forgetting, dream-first pretraining protects nothing (retention $1.71$/$1.80$, slightly worse than wake) while replaying old data during the conflict halves it ($0.91$/$1.07$). And \textbf{calibration has headroom exactly where replay, not dreaming, reaches it}: under observation noise the uncertainty head's coverage collapses ($0.19$--$0.61$; a terminal-event coverage hole opens---$0.0$ on every arm), capacity partially rescues it ($d_h{=}256$: $0.85$), and the three-arm read-your-dreams re-test closed with the instrument defect and seed split registered above (\S\ref{sec:neg}, A5). Four independent lines---behavioral transfer, semantic maintenance, switching tolerance, forgetting protection---now agree on the same division of labor.

\subsection{The external yardstick: parameter-matched autoregression}\label{sec:external}

The natural objection to everything above is architectural: perhaps any small recurrent model would show the same pattern, and the latent-space design is incidental. We ran the control twice, both times \emph{lockstep}---the two models consume bit-identical batches every cycle, at matched parameter counts (a hand-built causal transformer, context 8, $-0.1\%$/$-4.6\%$ vs.\ \som{} at 16K on the two worlds) and matched gradient steps. The readout is decode $R^2$ against a copy-frame baseline, evaluated on frozen episode sets.

\textbf{One step, teacher-forced}: the transformer wins by ${\sim}25$pp on both worlds and both seeds ($98$--$99$ vs.\ $69$--$74$ on the structured world, where copy explains only $16$--$30\%$). No surprise---the baseline's loss \emph{is} this readout, while \som{}'s observation head is a low-weight auxiliary of a latent-space objective; the comparison is honestly biased against us, and that is the point of running it. \textbf{Open loop} ($k{=}16$, each model fed its own predictions; \som{} from a grounded initial state, the transformer through its sliding 8-frame window) removes the excuse: the transformer still wins, by $51$ and $82$pp ($R^2$ at $k{=}16$: $60$/$89$ vs.\ $9$/$7$). At this scale the external-baseline question is closed: \emph{a parameter-matched autoregressor dominates both teacher-forced one-step and multi-step open-loop observation prediction}. Two residues survive, both registered: \som{}'s error compounds more slowly (MSE$_{16}$/MSE$_1$ $= 4.4$--$4.9\times$ vs.\ the transformer's $5.8$--$6.8\times$---the $O(1)$-state model degrades more gracefully per compounded step), and both models beat frame-copy at every horizon. What this bounds is the \emph{observation-prediction} framing of the ``thinking in latent space'' claim, not latent prediction itself; the registered open question is whether any regime exists at this scale where the latent substrate wins on its own terms.

\section{Related work}

World models with latent imagination~\cite{hafner2023} train policies on self-generated rollouts; \som{} differs in training the \emph{world model itself} before its first real step, under a developmental schedule. JEPA~\cite{lecun2022,assran2023} and latent-reasoning lines~\cite{hao2024,lcms} predict in representation space; we adopt the objective wholesale. Our growth operator follows Net2Net function-preserving widening~\cite{wei2016}; progressive growing has a long history~\cite{karras2018}. Sleep-inspired ML ranges from wake-sleep algorithms~\cite{hinton1995} to replay for continual learning~\cite{shin2017}; \som{} imports the vocabulary (homeostasis~\cite{tononi2014}, reverse learning~\cite{crick1983}) as engineering hypotheses subject to the same ablations as everything else. Finally, the pre-registration discipline follows clinical-trial practice; to our knowledge its combination with a public per-run registry is uncommon in small-scale ML research, and we found it changes what one is willing to claim.

\section{Conclusion}

A two-day, single-GPU project; five replications of a $60\%$ interaction saving; three independent measurements of where that saving ends; three pre-registered negatives kept on the books; a model that, left alone overnight, grew itself, hit its own wall, and learned to restore its last healthy self---and then, on a repaired instrument, the same question asked again and answered more precisely: the saving lives only where capacity is binding, and replay is the load-bearing component. The boundary was then measured from both sides---four worlds against the architecture (saturation at ${\sim}$3pp; the world family is the binding constraint)---and the non-stationary axis, closed once and re-opened by retracting our own closure, was finally \emph{measured}: semantics establish slowly (capacity and reward weight set the rate), re-invert quickly, and decay under switching with that same time constant---beyond birth, nothing depends on how the model slept. The final controls closed the remaining flanks: forgetting is real but replay-protected, not dream-protected; calibration headroom under noise is real but dream-reading does not reach it; and a parameter-matched autoregressor, run lockstep, wins every observation-prediction contest at this scale---leaving the latent substrate what it demonstrably has: a slower-compounding state, a cheaper birth, and a registry that kept score throughout. Along the way the paper retracts one of its own registered conclusions (an expressivity ceiling that turned out to be a one-layer fix) and logs the retraction with the same discipline as the original claim. We offer \som{} less as an architecture than as a working argument that the unit of progress in small-scale ML research can be the \emph{registered, falsifiable, retained claim}---including the ones that die, and the ones we kill ourselves.
